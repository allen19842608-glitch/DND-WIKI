## Tarokka and the Mists

Tarokka fortune-telling cards (detailed in the {@book appendix|RHW|6}) have special relationships with domains and their Darklords.

### Tarokka Mist Talismans

Each Domain of Dread is aligned with a tarokka card. A character with a tarokka deck can use it to travel between domains. While in the Mists, the deck's bearer can draw a random card from the deck; that card serves as a Mist talisman (see the {@area introduction|008|x}), allowing travel to the domain it is aligned with. A tarokka deck can be used in this way once every 24 hours. A list of domains and their aligned tarokka cards appears later in {@book this book|RHW|2|Random Domains of Dread}.

### Darklords and the Tarokka

Every Domain of Dread has a tarokka card that opposes it—one hinting at the truth of the domain's Darklord. If a domain's opposed tarokka card is drawn during a tarokka reading in that domain, that domain's Darklord becomes aware of the reading and its participants. When this occurs, the Darklord can choose to see through the eyes of the tarokka user and speak through them for 1 minute. The Darklord might make their presence known, level a threat, or misinterpret the cards. A tarokka reader has no memory of what a Darklord uses them to say.

In Barovia, the night is a curse. As darkness falls, wicked souls slip from the spires of Castle Ravenloft to work the will of an immortal overlord. This is the realm of the vampire {@creature Strahd von Zarovich|RHW|Count Strahd von Zarovich}, whose depravities have doomed him and his land to endless cycles of obsession and despair.

The howls of wolves and shrieks of ravens echo through the dismal valleys and oppressive forests of Barovia. In isolated communities, superstitious villagers find the brightness in their lives smothered by dread of their aloof overlord and other festering evils. These Barovians fear the night, as agents of the devil Strahd watch in the dark and claim whatever he desires. Yet few in Barovia realize their torments have played out again and again. In every generation, echoes of Strahd's past reemerge, compelling and confounding the Darklord as he endlessly obsesses over the one victim who's eluded him for centuries.

### Locations in Barovia

Barovia is a land of long shadows where ancient wildernesses strangle the ruins of a near-forgotten empire. Mysteries pervade the domain, and those who leave the relative safety of Barovia's dismal settlements enter perilous lands where mortals aren't welcome.

The village of Barovia is described below, and other notable locations are summarized on the {@book Barovia Locations table|RHW|2|Barovia Locations}.

#### Village of Barovia

Lying in the shadow of Castle Ravenloft, the village of Barovia teeters on the brink of ruin. Its few residents rarely venture from their homes, suspecting their neighbors of wickedness and fearing evils in the night. Their dread is largely justified, as wolves, ghosts, and vampire spawn haunt the town, and many locals have been consumed by callousness or wicked temptations.

A rare exception to the village's cold desperation can be found at the local tavern, the Blood of the Vine. Those few who oppose Strahd's evil congregate here—under the covert oversight of the count's agents.

### Strahd von Zarovich

{@i First of the Darklords}

I am the Ancient. I am the Land. My beginnings are lost in the darkness of the past. I am not dead. Nor am I alive. I am undead, forever.

Count Strahd von Zarovich has lost all he ever desired—over and over again. Tormented by the Dark Powers, Strahd is haunted by visions of his lost family and tarnished nobility. The count spends long years brooding in the depths of Castle Ravenloft, but a reincarnation of his obsession appears every generation: Tatyana. Each time Tatyana's reincarnation emerges, Strahd seeks to undo the failures of his past and find a way to win her love. Time and time again, Strahd's heartless, domineering nature causes his bloody history to repeat itself and drives Tatyana beyond his clutches.

A severe, aristocratic figure, the count has the bearing of a former soldier and the impatience of one used to being obeyed. He feels little but occasionally embraces the novelty of toying with amusing strangers or breaking the spirits of his enemies. During his unnaturally long existence, he has learned many occult secrets and mysteries of the Domains of Dread. Strahd's dignified bearing crumbles when someone breaks his laws or denies him what he desires. In such cases, Strahd makes bloody examples of his opponents, drawing out his vengeance into campaigns of terror.

#### Strahd von Zarovich

#### Strahd's Tale

Count Strahd von Zarovich was a ruthless conqueror. Retiring after years of war, Strahd settled in the beautiful valley where he'd won his greatest victory. There, in the way of his ancestors, he spilled his blood into the earth, sealing a pact between himself and the land. In honor of his parents, King Barov and Queen Ravenovia, he named the valley Barovia and constructed his home, Castle Ravenloft.

Strahd invited his few remaining family members to live with him at Castle Ravenloft and eventually was joined by his younger brother, Sergei. Sergei was everything Strahd wasn't—youthful, empathic, and warm. Soon after he arrived, the younger von Zarovich and a local villager named Tatyana fell in love. Strahd resented his brother, even more so after meeting Tatyana and being charmed by her pure kindness. Obsessed, Strahd sought to woo her but was rebuffed. Unwilling to accept her wishes or the idea of Sergei as his better, Strahd made a pact with ancient evil forces to regain his vitality and ensnare Tatyana's heart.

The day of Sergei and Tatyana's wedding, Strahd murdered Sergei and, to seal his pact, drank his brother's blood. He revealed his new might to Tatyana, expecting to enthrall her. Instead, horrified by Sergei's murder, Tatyana fled Strahd, ultimately leaping from the height of Castle Ravenloft and vanishing into the Mists. At the same time, traitors from among the castle's guards and wedding's guests rose up to assassinate Strahd. Despite countless wounds, Strahd didn't die. His bargain with the Dark Powers had made Strahd the multiverse's first vampire. A night of slaughter followed; when dawn touched the parapets of Castle Ravenloft, no living soul survived within. But Strahd remained.

Strahd's villainy and his connection to his land drew all of Barovia into the grip of the Dark Powers, which transplanted the vampire and his realm into the Shadowfell. This began a succession of torments that plague the count still. The Dark Powers' attentions have spilled forth to drag others into the Mists as well, giving rise to the Domains of Dread.

#### Strahd's Lairs

While the notorious Castle Ravenloft is Strahd's home, it isn't his only redoubt in Barovia. Multiple bastions of evil serve as sanctuaries for the count and his servants, should fate demand.

##### Castle Ravenloft

Castle Ravenloft is Strahd von Zarovich's accursed sanctuary. Tormented spirits and Strahd's minions haunt the vast castle, each serving the count's schemes, reflecting some aspect of his depraved past, or protecting his coffin in the castle catacombs. Mementos of lost times and fallen heroes lie throughout the count's home, some of which might hold the key to his undoing. The adventure {@adventure Curse of Strahd|CoS} details Castle Ravenloft.

##### Von Zarovich Sanctuaries

Hidden across Barovia lie secret sanctuaries known only to Strahd and his servants. These crypt-like boltholes are hidden from the sun and exist primarily to shelter the count should he require. Over the centuries, Strahd has granted trusted servants access to some of these sanctuaries and used others in plots or experiments that he keeps distant from Castle Ravenloft. Still others Strahd has forgotten, allowing other evils to fester there. An example of one of these sites is depicted on {@book map 2.2|RHW|2|Map 2.2: Blood of the Vine Tavern} and has the following features:

- Count's Coffin

- The Lord's Presence

- Servants' Space

### Adventures in Barovia

Adventures set in Barovia involve oppressive, gothic menace; suspicion; and the legendary threat of Strahd von Zarovich. Use this section to develop horror adventures in this domain.

#### Horror in Barovia

Adventures in Barovia often include the following elements.

##### The Ancient, The Land

Strahd remains bound to Barovia and it to him in a way few rulers or Darklords understand. He knows when any creature enters or dies violently within Barovia, and he takes offense when someone slays his servants or the domain's wolves. Strahd can manifest a variety of dramatic effects, such as casting his voice on the wind, making his visage appear in the clouds, or changing the weather. These effects don't aid him in combat, but they contribute to making him feel like an oppressive and ever-present threat.

##### Incarnations of the Past

The subject of Strahd's obsession, Tatyana, perpetually reincarnates among the people of Barovia. No matter what form she takes, the Darklord unceasingly seeks her, determined to possess her and make her part of an idealized fantasy of his past. Tatyana's incarnation might be anyone, including a player character. If her incarnation is an NPC, roll on the Tatyana's Reincarnation and Connection to Strahd tables to inspire a new chapter in the count's tale of obsession.

##### Roots of Evil

As the first Domain of Dread, Barovia might serve an important role in the metaphysical underpinnings of the setting. Some Darklords might have emerged from the Mists first in Barovia, encountered Strahd, and found their ways to their own domains later. Others might have a particular interest in Barovia and in undoing Strahd's connection to his land. Consider these possibilities when developing plots involving conflicts between Darklords or escaping the Mists, as outlined in {@book chapter 3|RHW|3}.

##### Signs of the Count

Servants of Strahd are everywhere in Barovia. While in the domain, characters experience regular reminders that the Darklord watches them—and that they are unwelcome. Roll on the Strahd's Presence table to inspire such encounters.

The noble families of Borca engage in an endless war of conspiracies and lethal betrayals as they pursue influence, thrills, and age-old rivalries. People here value guile and callousness, and none embody those traits better than the domain's two Darklords: the genius poisoner {@creature Ivana Boritsi|RHW} and the childishly cruel stalker {@creature Ivan Dilisnya|RHW}.

Beyond the fashionable holdings of Borca's elite, commoners struggle against crime, poverty, and starvation. In scattered villages and tenement-filled cities, locals view nobles as celebrities, and the commoners' idealized vision of noble life leads them to mimic the aristocrats' callousness and appetite for empty fads. Desperate commoners are tempted into debt and depravity in pursuit of nobles' favor and passing fancies. Those who don't bend to the whims of Borca's elite face humiliation before they're crushed.

### Domain Features

Borca is known for the following features:

- Two Darklords

- Desperate Sycophants

### Locations in Borca

The impoverished lands of Borca languish in the shadow of opulent noble estates and garish monuments to greed. The exploited land bears the scars of age-old rivalries and the hiding places of noble secrets.

The capital of Levkarest is described below, and other notable locations are summarized on the {@book Borca Locations table|RHW|2|Borca Locations}.

#### Levkarest

Borca's capital of Levkarest stands at the pinnacle of culture and ostentatious power. Here, lesser nobles pursue fickle dramas and vogue trends while the populace labors amid workhouses and cultlike fashion houses. Commoners glimpse this world of toxic glamor only from afar or when nobles exploit street-level fads.

At the city's heart rises the People's Parliament, the land's ineffective seat of government. Here, unpopular members of noble families squabble over meaningless legislation. The nation's true rulers conduct business on noble estates, in avant-garde clubs, and in the offices of faceless mercantile ventures.

Near the People's Parliament stands the Great Cathedral. Within, pious commoners and ostentatious aristocrats attend ceremonies sacred to the deity Ezra. Locals say the cathedral was created by nobles who believed they'd earned the ire of some great evil. Centuries later, these families still support the church, transactionally investing in dubious supernatural insurance.

### Ivana Boritsi

{@i Darklord of Poisonous Ambition}

Your kind can't possibly fathom how much this sort of interruption costs the House of Boritsi. Don't make it worse by being tedious.

Ivana Boritsi tires of playing games no one realizes she's already won. The head of Borca's most influential family, Ivana effortlessly manipulates all aspects of society. Her servants, employees, and fractious cousins prove endlessly unreliable, forcing her to attend personally to tedious duties and social functions to maintain her power.

Ivana looks eternally youthful, causing some to doubt her competence and attribute her success to her elder cousin, {@creature Ivan Dilisnya|RHW}. Perpetually bored with and disappointed by predictable people, she takes refuge in her alchemical studies and her gardens of poisonous plants. Her vast knowledge of toxins makes her a peerless poisoner. She uses her creations to make people more pliable and to assassinate those she deems too boorish to live. Secretly, Ivana seeks someone who can match her intellect and relieve her boredom, but she quickly grows weary of even the most charming and clever souls.

#### Ivana's Tale

The firstborn of a fantastically wealthy family, Ivana Boritsi was clever, poised, and—in the eyes of her father, Klaus—utterly unfit to lead the family. Driven to prove her father wrong and encouraged by her mother, Camille, Ivana learned every aspect of her family's vast perfume and medicine business.

Her focus on her goals faltered when she met Pieter, a skilled chemist in her family's service. Camille recognized her daughter's budding romance and saw the lowborn Pieter as a threat to the girl's prospects. Camille seduced Pieter and arranged for her daughter to discover them, intent on convincing her to forsake this and all future romances.

Ivana took a different lesson from the experience. Realizing the depths of her family's depravity, she poisoned her mother and her callous younger siblings with toxins of her own design. None suspected Ivana, but soon after, Klaus summoned her. Having lost his sons, Klaus named her cousin, Ivan Dilisnya, his heir. Ivana was the last to be informed, her father having already announced his decision to the estate and sent a messenger to inform the Dilisnyas.

Having expected this, Ivana had poisoned the perfumery on the estate's grounds, turning its far-ranging smells deadly. Ivana laughed as toxic mist poured across the estate. As she sipped the antidote, she watched Klaus and the manor's staff die choking amid a violet fog. When the poison cleared, the Mists had surrounded the land of Borca (for details on Ivana's {@area domain|0fc|x} and {@area lair|0fd|x}, see "{@area Ivan Dilisnya|0fa|x}").

##### Ivana Boritsi

### Ivan Dilisnya

{@i Darklord of Childish Cruelty}

Oh ho oh! What succulent insipidity I see in those lumpish eyes! What divine folly! I swoon, I croon, I know! You'll be a new favorite!

Ivan Dilisnya—a spoiled, selfish attention seeker—has the tastes of a child but a withered wreck of a body. Borcan society widely regards this recluse as an innovator and a genius. In truth, he is neither. Ivan is a wicked and off-putting manipulator who, despite his appearance and intellect, affects childlike behaviors and speech patterns for attention. He is alone—despite having been born with everything—and the solitude terrifies him.

By staying out of sight or using deceptive correspondence, Ivan makes his targets doubt reality and overestimate his control. He manipulates people into performing pointless errands for him or spying on his cousin, {@creature Ivana Boritsi|RHW}, whom he believes mocks him and has caused his aged, squalid state.

Ivan surrounds himself with dangerous clockwork toys and fawning, mechanical servants. While he goes through the motions of crafting such devices, the Dark Powers provide him with any creation he desires, no matter how bizarre.

#### Ivan's Tale

Treachery is tradition for the Dilisnya family. The land of Borca originates from the same world as Barovia's conquerors, the von Zaroviches. Dilisnyas were at Castle Ravenloft the night of Strahd von Zarovich's transformation.

Ages later, the siblings Boris and Camille Dilisnya plotted to steal the prestige of their cousins, the Boritsis. Camille manipulated her way into the Boritsi family, while Boris prepared his son, Ivan, to act as head of a united Boritsi–Dilisnya house.

Despite abundant opportunities, Ivan resented his family's plans for him and grew to embody puerile vice and impulses. His family's wealth and doting servants exacerbated this problem, even as Ivan fixated on childish toys and behaviors. Frequent "accidents" transpired around him, forcing his family to cover up the maimings and deaths of numerous pets and servants. Only his beloved sister, Kristina, remained immune to his wicked tricks. Although Ivan's family grew unsettled by Ivan, they sheltered the future head of their house, reshaping their estate and investing in whimsical diversions for him.

On the same night Ivana Boritsi poisoned her family, Ivan learned his parents intended to send Kristina to a prestigious boarding school. Screams, cruel cackling, and clockwork screeching filled the estate that night. When it ended, Ivan Dilisnya was the last living member of his family, and the Mists had closed around Borca.

### Adventures in Borca

Adventures set in Borca involve intrigue between petty nobles, family secrets, manipulation, and deception. Use this section to develop horror adventures in this domain.

#### Horror in Borca

Adventures in Borca often include the following elements.

##### Nobles of Borca

Twelve depraved families rule over every aspect of life in Borca. Each is a core family of fifteen or fewer irredeemable individuals united around the head of the house, who tyrannically controls the clan's myriad ventures. Servants and business representatives across the domain support these core families, as do members of branch families—lackeys with different surnames who are distantly related to the core family or indentured to them.

The Noble Families of Borca table details the domain's most powerful families.

##### Supernatural Intrigue

Magic and the supernatural heighten the gilded depravities of Borca. When planning adventures in Borca, consider including the following elements.

###### Curses and Poison

{@book Chapter 3|XDMG|2} of the {@book Dungeon Master's Guide|XDMG} describes curses, magical contagions, and poison. Villains in Borca might use these or adjusted versions of your own design—perhaps with signature cosmetic effects.

###### Monsters

Monsters can serve as threats but also agents of noble houses. {@variantrule Shape-shifting|XPHB} creatures such as {@creature Imp|XMM|Imps} and {@creature Wererat|XMM|Wererats} excel at infiltration, while {@creature Doppelganger|XMM|Doppelgangers} and {@creature Succubus|XMM|Succubi} might pose as nobles, innocents, or trusted allies. Creatures like {@creature Ghost|XMM|Ghosts} and {@creature Intellect Devourer|XMM|Intellect Devourers} might control individuals, making them behave in unusual ways. Choose a single monster, and consider how you might make it central to a devious noble's plot.

###### Spells and Magic Items

Don't try to account for or incorporate all magic into adventures involving supernatural intrigue. Choose a single spell—such as {@spell Clairvoyance|XPHB}, {@spell Geas|XPHB}, or {@spell Speak with Dead|XPHB}—and consider how to make it central to a plot involving secrets and mysterious revelations. Consider doing the same with a magic item such as a {@item Cloak of the Bat|XDMG}, a {@item Hag Eye|XDMG}, or {@item Sending Stones|XDMG}. Characters should earn rewards for using their own magic in clever ways.

### Azalin Rex

#### Azalin's Tale

Firan Zal'honan was a noble and heir to the city-state of Knurl on the world of Oerth. A student of magic, Firan adopted the title "Azal'Lan," meaning "wizard-king," when he came to power. Firan's rule was marked by harsh laws and violent expansion, but his people flourished, as their lives were filled with magical wonders.

As Firan aged, he looked toward his succession. His son, Irik, was generous and charming but, in Firan's eyes, soft and unfit to rule. No matter how Firan tried to teach his son of the world's harsh realities, Irik's kind spirit wouldn't bend to his father's cynical outlook. This difference came to a head when Firan caught Irik freeing political prisoners. Firan could have ignored the crime or pardoned his son. Instead, Firan personally wielded the axe during Irik's execution.

In the years that followed, Firan was tormented by his son's death, but he also became convinced that only he was fit to rule. Researching the mysteries of death, he discovered the secret to becoming a lich. After embracing the transition into undeath, Firan ruled for decades more, increasing Knurl's power and earning himself many foes. Eventually, tales of magic that might restore Irik lured Firan from his home. During his search, a band of mercenaries ambushed the ruler. Firan fled into the mist to avoid the assassins. When the mist cleared, Firan found himself in {@book Barovia|RHW|2}.

In Barovia, peasants Firan encountered mistook his title and called him Azalin. Soon, Azalin encountered Barovia's ruler, {@creature Strahd von Zarovich|RHW}. The two came to respect each other, and Azalin taught Strahd much of the way of magic. Together, the pair learned a great deal about the nature of the Domains of Dread and made attempts to escape. Their plots failed, though, and resulted in other realms—such as the domain of {@book Mordent|RHW|2}—being drawn into Barovia's orbit. Eventually, the two tyrants' respect soured into loathing. Before Azalin and Strahd could destroy each other, Azalin entered the Mists, and the domain of Darkon spread before him.

#### Jagged Coast

Stark vistas of sheer cliffs, mysterious islands, and knotted forests bleed from one to the next along the Jagged Coast. Since Azalin's disappearance, the Darklord's secret police have maintained order here—obeying not the commands of the missing king, but rather the will of their regional leader, Alcio Metus.

##### Kargat

Azalin's secret police, the Kargat, has long enforced the Darklord's edicts and smothered any ember of rebellion in Darkon. Those "disappeared" by the Kargat are never seen again.

While all Darkon fears these dark-cloaked spies, few realize the Kargat is largely composed of vampires and other shape-shifting creatures. Members operate in cells assigned to particular tasks or locations, with each reporting to a regional leader. The highest-ranking members reported to Azalin. Serving the Kargat are the Kargatane, a cultlike rank of living informants and initiates who spy on their neighbors in hopes of earning supernatural power and induction into the larger organization.

The Hour of Ascension threw the Kargat into disorder. Taking advantage of this opportunity, Alcio Metus (Medium, Neutral Evil {@creature Vampire|XMM}), a Kargat leader and criminal kingpin in Martira Bay, slew her peers and took over the western branch of the group. From the Black Tower in Martira Bay, Alcio spreads her Kargat spies across region and beyond.

##### Martira Bay

A foggy port on the Sea of Souls, Martira Bay is a cosmopolitan hub of trade and ideas. The populace prides itself on its worldliness and believes that the best of Darkon and lands beyond passes through the city. Martira Bay's people appreciate reason and tangibility, and many have never left the city. This makes many residents incredulous of magic and the supernatural, even as séances and spiritualist practices flourish in posh salons.

Martira Bay is infamous for sensationalist murders. Newspapers such as {@i The Bullseye} update their devoted readership on the grisly details of sprees committed by killers such as the Midnight Slasher, the Spider, and the Weeping Woman. The overwhelmed Martira Bay constabulary works to counter these killers, as do professional detective agencies including the Ray Detectives (founded by Alanik Ray; see {@area chapter 3|2f3|x}) and the Dusklight Detectives.

#### Lychgate

Lychgate hides the magically shattered remains of Castle Avernus. Whatever ongoing magical disaster befell the castle during the Hour of Ascension has locked its ruins in time, freezing it in a semi-exploded state. While Castle Avernus remains the lair of some of Azalin's most dangerous servants, the surrounding wilderness has become overrun by shuffling corpses and mournful spirits drawn to the castle's strange magic. #### Mistlands

Even before the Hour of Ascension, western Darkon was a land of magical mysteries and strange creatures. No longer fearing the Darklord's magic, ancient evils have crept from the shadows to terrorize and control the scattered communities.

##### Rexcrown

Residents of Rexcrown consider themselves the only true Darkonians due to their proximity to the capital, Il Aluk. Despite that prestige, Rexcrown is covered in deadly swamps, depleted mountains, and fields that produce disturbingly malformed crops.

{@i Darklord Who Wars Against the Dead}

General of a demoralized occupying army, Vladeska Drakov rules what little of Falkovnia hasn't been overrun by the dead. #### Vladeska Drakov

#### Drakov's Tale

In the world of her birth, Vladeska Drakov was known as the Crimson Falcon, leader of a peerless mercenary army called the Falcon's Talons. In a land of bitterly feuding royal families, she and her troops executed a thousand ancient grudges and petty atrocities. Drakov amassed a fortune and planned to retire young and buy a title and a realm to rule.

The sacking of Yeivere changed her plans. During that city's razing, Drakov's mercenaries went too far. The Falcon's Talons killed a unique soul during the slaughter. None can say who the soul was—a royal, a saint, a witch, an angel—but with its death, the world turned against them.

The ruling families united to hunt the Talons. Thanks to her tactical genius, Drakov repelled her pursuers and went on the offensive. One by one she attacked her former benefactors, burning villages, pressing citizens into service, and impaling anyone with a drop of noble blood. Over years of work, Drakov forged a bloody empire. But while sacking the burning city of her last remaining foe, Drakov and her troops were engulfed by smoke. When it cleared, everything they knew was gone.

Transported to an unfamiliar land, Drakov and her troops wasted little time subjugating it. This unprepared realm fell quickly, almost willingly. On taking the city of Lekar, Drakov named herself ruler of her new empire: Falkovnia.

However, under a moonless sky, the dead rose against Drakov, claws scraping against Lekar's walls before alarms could be raised. Drakov's brilliant and desperate strategies eventually turned back the zombies during the days-long First Siege of Lekar, but victory came at the cost of most of her veteran troops.

Since then, Drakov has nearly lost her nation. Every moon, a new desperate battle unfolds. She prepares ceaselessly for each one, certain of her ultimate victory but doubtful of her troops' mettle. With threats and public impalings, she keeps her people more terrified of her than of the undead. And with every new moon, she faces an unconquerable foe, never revealing a secret only she knows: that each zombie bears the face of an innocent her soldiers once put to the sword.

#### Ankhtepot's Tale

In the ages since, Ankhtepot hasn't forged the land of his ambitions. His servants scheme against him, his people don't flourish, and his monuments crumble to dust in mere centuries. ##### Eyes of Hazlik

Hazlik spies on his domain's inhabitants using the Eye of Hazlik. This eyelike sigil adorns buildings, clothing, talismans, and creatures across the domain. ##### Hazlan Wild Magic

As a result of unchecked arcane experimentation, magic functions erratically in Hazlan. Whenever a creature in Hazlan expends a spell slot to cast a spell or activates a magic item, it rolls {@dice 1d10}. On a 1, it unleashes a random magical effect. Roll on the {@table Wild Magic Surge|XPHB|Wild Magic Surge table} in chapter 3 of the {@book Player's Handbook|XPHB} to determine the effect. Consider describing these results in unsettling ways that don't change their effects, as in these examples:

- Summoned creatures use the proscribed stat blocks but are cosmetically warped to resemble undead or aberrant creatures.

- Effects that teleport the caster relocate the caster into a misty expanse full of threatening shapes.

- Effects on creatures cause unsettling sensations, such as the feeling of earwigs wriggling beneath one's skin or one's teeth struggling free.

Anyone who openly wears an Eye of Hazlik can choose not to roll on the Wild Magic Surge table in this way once per day.

Dozens of mages serve as Hazlik's apprentices. This creates a cadre of Hazlani mages who recklessly seek to impress Hazlik by the following means, among others:

- Changing the nature of reality to suit their whims

- Creating monuments in Hazlik's honor

- Devising new magic items, spells, or supernatural materials

- Forging unique magical servants for Hazlik

- Stealing rare magic items

- Unleashing dramatic displays of magical might

Hazlik's current favorite apprentices include the masked wizard Yhal the Skygazer, the shadow-cloaked Ruzelo, and the daring genius Eleni.

Hidden amid the Domains of Dread lies a hideous realm defiant of reason and smothered by the Mists. From a violent, murky sea rises a crescent of shattered peaks, mangled spires broken like the jaws of some slain thalassic titan. From noxious fumaroles, miasmic pillars lurch from this accursed isle into a sky inconsistently lit by a distant, drowned light.

Little evidence remains of this settlement's residents, but at its edge lies a toppled sign bearing a word in Common that gives the island its name: Innsmouth.

Despite its forsaken appearance, the isle of Innsmouth isn't abandoned. Cults vie for control of the island, among them the servants of Great Cthulhu, a harbinger of world-rending aberrance. At the island's hidden heart, {@creature Cthulhu|RHW} lies dreaming, its alien mind reaching across the Domains of Dread and beyond, drawing to it those who might rouse its full might and unleash it from its prison in the Mists.

### Cthulhu

{@i Darklord What Eternal Lies}

Ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn.

Cthulhu was—and inevitably will be once more—a threat to the entire multiverse. For untold ages, the Dark Powers have bound this harbinger of doom. Unlike the other prisoners of the Domains of Dread, Cthulhu isn't plagued by ironic torments or thwarted ambitions. Rather, this ageless terror remains physically bound and can exert only a fraction of its terrible might. Nevertheless, Cthulhu's dreams slip beyond its prison, manipulating minds and orchestrating events to ensure its release and a new epoch of multiversal doom.

#### Cthulhu

#### Cthulhu's Tale

Spawned beyond the borders of the multiverse in realities where time and life have little worth, Cthulhu is a servant of elder evils and a harbinger of their inevitable ruin. For untold eons, Cthulhu's will influenced receptive minds across the multiverse, reaching out in dreams and using them to open gateways to the Far Realm. Through such portals, Cthulhu visited the Material Plane, corrupted and laid waste to civilizations, and erected city-size monuments to its mighty predecessors.

Such distortion and defilement didn't go unnoticed by the Dark Powers. Multiple times the Mists closed around dread Cthulhu, and multiple times the terror evaded them. It was drawn into the Shadowfell when an extinct cabal of mages summoned it onto a sepulchral world of the Material Plane during a once-in-an-era alignment of eclipses. With the magic of the Shadowfell enhanced, the Mists rose and drew in Cthulhu. Raging and grasping, Cthulhu dragged with it remnants of past conquests and violent minds linked to its own. These disparate nightmares became the foundation of its prison: a surreal island hidden amid the Domains of Dread.

Deceptively beautiful, Kalakeri is a land of verdant rainforests and a web of rivers and lakes known as the Backwaters. At the height of its prosperity, Kalakeri was a robust center of art, commerce, and religion, with merchants spreading wild tales of the land, using fanciful names such as Sri Raji and the Steaming Lands. Now Kalakeri is a conflict-torn shadow of its past glory. The remnants of the Vasavadan dynasty—three heirs transformed into monsters by their depravity and hatred—trap the land in an endless cycle of war.

The people of Kalakeri live in fear of {@creature Ramya Vasavadan|RHW|Maharani Ramya Vasavadan}, the domain's Darklord, as well as her treacherous siblings, Arijani and Reeva. The Darklord's curse manifests both in her deathless desire to rule Kalakeri and in her siblings' endless rebellions. Both sides escalate their atrocities against one another, drawing the powerful and the innocent alike into their squabbles. As the maharani's siblings court allies, the Darklord's forces hunt and execute renegades, adding skulls to the grim Tower of Traitors.

### Locations in Kalakeri

Ancient structures adorned with the artistry of past dynasties stand alongside modern buildings in Kalakeri's capital, Jadurai. The Darklord's skeletal soldiers build the tower as part of her plan to rid Kalakeri of evil and receive the gods' blessings.

### Ramya Vasavadan

{@i Darklord Perpetually Denied Control}

Are you loyal to your maharani? Or are you yet more rebel skulls to add to the Tower of Traitors?

Ramya Vasavadan desperately fights to claim and hold power over Kalakeri in a brutal cycle of victory and loss with her equally uncompromising siblings. Unlike most Darklords, Ramya struggles to control her domain, leading her to seek allies in opposing her siblings and their treacherous allies.

In her true form, Ramya is an undead horror, but illusions mask her monstrous appearance. Her true nature is revealed by her chilled skin and her reflection, which always appears withered and terrifying.

Although Ramya's siblings, Arijani and Reeva, aren't Darklords of Kalakeri, they motivate the rebellions in Kalakeri and Ramya's torments. This monstrous pair is detailed in "{@area Arijani and Reeva|1cb|x}."

#### Ramya Vasavadan

#### Ramya's Tale

The Vasavadan dynasty has ruled Kalakeri since ancient times. Ramya Vasavadan was handpicked by her father to succeed him, but on the bleak day of his death, Ramya's brother, Arijani, declared himself the new maharaja.

Ramya refused to yield to her brother, and with her allies, she captured and prepared to execute him. But Ramya's sister, Reeva, counseled mercy. Instead, Ramya imprisoned Arijani and ascended as Kalakeri's maharani, Queen of Kings.

In the years that followed, Ramya ruled justly. But in the shadows, Reeva plotted with rebels, who eventually freed Arijani and spurred nobles across the nation to revolt.

Regretting her mercy, Ramya sent her army to crush the rebels and mercilessly execute the treacherous. Reeva and Arijani combated Ramya's superior forces with ambushes and deceptions. Once again exploiting Ramya's sympathies, the pair tricked Ramya, invaded the Cerulean Citadel, and executed their sister by strangling. With her final gasps, Ramya cursed her brother and sister, calling them bloodthirsty beasts. Soon after, a terrible monsoon struck Kalakeri, bringing with it the Mists.

The storm lashed Kalakeri for weeks. At its height, Ramya returned from the dead and called on those who had fallen in her service. Loyal in death, a corpse legion joined the revitalized maharani, besieged the Cerulean Citadel, and slaughtered Arijani and Reeva. Ramya then stole their skulls and built them into the foundations of what would become the Tower of Traitors.

Arijani and Reeva's plots didn't end with their lives. They too were reborn, transformed into monstrous reflections of their ravenous desires.

Now in the Land of the Mists, the conflict gripping Kalakeri continues, trapping the nation in an endless war and forcing its people to swear loyalty to monsters.

A domain of amoral science, Lamordia shudders amid the icy environment and terror of its residents' creations. Revolutions of steam and steel spur Lamordia's people into frenzies of amoral invention. From industrialized communities and the iron walls of Ludendorf, the domain's largest city, flow streams of scrap and chemical poisons that taint the land and transform creatures into horrors. Yet, the domain's reckless scientists don't pursue just mechanized inventions—the greatest goal is reinvention of life itself, transmuting people into ideals and unlocking generations of potential. In the name of such ambitions, the depraved reshape life and unleash abominations. And no aspirations have unleashed greater evils than those of the domain's Darklord, {@creature Viktra Mordenheim|RHW|Dr. Viktra Mordenheim}.

### Locations in Lamordia

Lamordia is a frigid land. In the north, the Sea of Secrets is choked with ice, while the mountains known as the Sleeping Beast are never without snow. The cities of Ludendorf and Neufurchtenburg are detailed below, and other notable locations are summarized on the {@book Lamordia Locations table|RHW|2|Lamordia Locations}.

#### Ludendorf

The city of Ludendorf is a morass of steam-powered factories operating at the behest of morally bankrupt barons. Boiling sewers and steam tunnels thread the city's foundations, heating cramped tenements and powering cranes serving the city's fleet of ironclad ships. These ice-breaking, whaling vessels dare the Sea of Secrets to bring back massive beasts that provide vital food and fuel for the city.

At the city's heart, the unscrupulous Ludendorf University conducts risky investigations into such fields as alchemical combustion, automated ingenuity, chemical sentience, and speculative anatomy. The university continually needs new subjects for experiments and hires volunteers or pays individuals for "flesh rights"—ownership of their cadavers once they die. A secret society within Ludendorf University idolizes Viktra and follows the reckless paths laid by her work.

#### Neufurchtenburg

The smoking refineries of Neufurchtenburg process minerals harvested from the Sleeping Beast. These rare, little-understood elements are then shipped to Ludendorf's factories. Radiation-warped monsters from the nearby bogs frequently attack, disrupting operations, but the town's people use hulking clockwork machines, cutting-edge firearms, and destructive alchemy to fend off incursions.

### Viktra Mordenheim

{@i Darklord Who Pursues Perfection}

Ah. You're conscious. Good. Test your limbs, and get accustomed to your new body.

From her laboratory at Schloss Mordenheim, Dr. Viktra Mordenheim uses her scientific genius to create horrors and wonders. All manner of undead and constructs march from her laboratory, but such flawed creations don't satisfy Mordenheim. Rather, she seeks to re-create or reclaim her greatest creation, the Unbreakable Heart, a miraculous device that grants life to Mordenheim's elusive reanimated victim, Elise.

#### Viktra Mordenheim

#### Viktra's Tale

From an early age, Viktra Mordenheim sought to understand the complexities of anatomy. She pursued medicine not to aid patients but to reveal the secrets of life. Eventually, her research bent toward reanimating dead flesh and improving humanoid bodies. When Viktra began employing thieves to procure specimens for her tests, she met Elise, a beautiful resurrectionist whose spontaneity entranced the methodical surgeon. Viktra was smitten, and with Elise she discovered there might be more to life than chemical reactions.

When Elise started exhibiting signs of an incurable disease, Viktra hastened her experiments. On the doctor's operating table, victims were killed, returned to life, and died again as Viktra sought a breakthrough. When finally Elise passed away, Viktra refused to accept failure. The doctor worked feverishly crafting her masterpiece, an artificial organ she called the Unbreakable Heart. But as she fitted the device into Elise's corpse, constables burst into the lab and accused the doctor of numerous murders. As Viktra struggled against arrest, smoking chemicals, arcing electricity, and a strange mist filled the laboratory.

When the chaos cleared, Viktra awoke in Lamordia, an unfamiliar land where her genius was celebrated. Elise and the Unbreakable Heart were nowhere to be found, but the doctor soon heard rumors of a wondrous and terrifying figure who wandered the icy realm and whose chest glowed as though her heart were the sun. Ever since, Viktra has continued her experiments, striving to re-create her successes but failing every time. Between disappointments she searches for whatever Elise has become—and with her, the miracle of the Unbreakable Heart.

#### Mordentshire

Mordentshire is the largest community in Mordent. Sturdy fisherfolk work the town docks and, after dusk, fill crowded tavern common rooms. Cold winds frequently blow in from Arden Bay. When they stop, they're replaced by a shroud of fog the locals call "the breath of the dead."

Speak your piece, then hold your tongue. No fools are more insufferable than the living.

#### Wilfred's Tale

In life, Lord Wilfred Godefroy was an unremarkable minor aristocrat. He inherited the estate of Gryphon Hill near Mordentshire centuries ago by murdering his father and framing it as an accident. During a bloody night years later, the entire Godefroy family—Wilfred; his wife, Estelle; and their daughter, Penelope—died violently. But instead of passing on to the afterlife, the Godefroys' souls lingered at Gryphon Hill. In death, Wilfred is haunted by the ghosts of his family, recent and the past generations, who blame him for their deaths and the family's disgrace.

A generation ago, a mysterious alchemist created a device in Mordent called the {@area Apparatus|21b|x}. This magical machine had many fantastic powers, including the ability to open passages between the planes and separate a creature's good and evil natures into separate beings. The Apparatus drew multiple powerful beings to Mordent and eventually attracted the Mists themselves when the device malfunctioned, causing a deadly disaster. The Mists collected Mordent into the Domains of Dread, and the mysterious alchemist vanished. With the departure of mightier beings, Lord Godefroy remained as the land's most fearful villain. He inherited the mantle of Darklord of the new domain of Mordent.

Since then, Wilfred has claimed lordship of all who die in Mordent. He knows the location of the Apparatus and seeks to master its powers, but how to do so and the location of a vital piece of the machine—the Rod of Rastinon—both elude him. He seeks to blackmail anyone who might be able to reveal the device's secrets or find the missing rod. Wilfred forces Alice Weathermay, mayor of Mordentshire, to keep him appraised of useful visitors to the domain, by holding her dead husband, Daniel Foxgrove, hostage.

For the knights of the Shadowlands, the struggle with evil isn't a philosophical trial—it is an active war. East of the rugged Theospine Mountains spreads the peaceful realm of Nidala, where the virtuous knights of the Circle guard their lands against wickedness beyond and within. #### Ebonbane's Tale

Kateri Shadowborn was a great hero who, alongside her order of valorous knights called the Circle, spent her life in service of good. Among her greatest deeds was leading an army against a nation whose king had been possessed by a fiend. She defeated the fiend, though doing so caused her magic sword to shatter.

After a life of great deeds, Kateri Shadowborn retired to live in peace with her family at Shadowborn Manor. But the hero had made many enemies. Thinking her vulnerable, wicked cultists recovered the pieces of Kateri's broken sword and conjured the lingering malice of the evils she had thwarted. These the cultists forged into a terrible weapon, {@i Ebonbane}—and they became its first victims.

Animated by hatred for Kateri and her allies, {@i Ebonbane} took control of its creators and became a curse on the world. For years it worked to destroy what Kateri had created, ruin what she loved, and slaughter those who aided her. Many times the evil sword's wielder was captured, but none ever suspected that the weapon itself was behind the grisly crimes.

Finally, in the hands of an unassuming monk, {@i Ebonbane} found its way to Shadowborn Manor, where grim news had isolated Kateri and worn her down. Aged but still a mighty knight, Kateri recognized {@i Ebonbane}'s threat, and a great battle ensued. During the clash, Kateri kept {@i Ebonbane}'s attention and ensured her family and friends escaped the manor. When only the hero and the wicked sword remained, Kateri summoned her remaining might into a blow to destroy {@i Ebonbane}. But the wicked sword proved too powerful. Kateri's blade shattered, and {@i Ebonbane} struck down the legendary knight. Exalting in its victory, the evil weapon possessed Kateri's body and pursued her fleeing family. But as it did so, it witnessed its quarry fading into the rising Mists.

When the Mists receded, {@i Ebonbane} found itself in a realm where the residents regarded the ancestors of Kateri Shadowborn and her fellow knights as heroes. Thrilled to continue its spree, {@i Ebonbane} sought to leave Shadowborn Manor, but it had no one to carry it beyond the grounds. Now, the evil blade seethes in frustration, waiting for a worthy bearer to carry it into the world so it might destroy all the good that came from Kateri's work.

Hidden amid ruins spilling into the chasm is the home of Inza Kulchevich, a hateful witch who can speak to shadows and believes all virtue is a lie. Despite her cynicism, she might perform prophetic fortune-telling or share strange lore in return for her visitors' shadows.

### Lord Soth

{@i Darklord Cursed by Memories}

Explain why you have interrupted my reverie, or I will turn your waking hours into nightmares.

Lord Loren Soth seethes within a realm that mocks and frustrates him. Having known the rigid standards of knighthood and warfare, Soth finds Sithicus empty and chaotic, his people apathetic and undisciplined. The elven beauty that once captivated him lies ruined, the land burns with the flames of Krynn's Cataclysm, and the realm's shadows remind Soth of his past failures. Worse, no worthy foes exist to challenge him. So Soth broods on his throne in Nedragaard Keep, haunted by banshees that sing the tale of his disgrace and by rotted, undead copies of the knights who once served him.

Lord Soth cares nothing for his domain. But should noteworthy threats or remarkable individuals appear, he seeks to crush or enlist them. The only exception is the spirit of his treacherous former ally, Caradoc. Soth's undead servants and his werebadger seneschal, Azrael Dax, scour the land for the elusive ghost.

Soth shares his imprisonment in Sithicus with all in the domain by keeping its borders perpetually closed. Only those who rouse him to activity or invade Nedragaard Keep might convince him to let them escape. This risks provoking the mighty death knight's wrath, but Soth still thinks of himself as a true knight—he might assist those who present themselves as honorable warriors or show knowledge of his home (see "{@area Influencing Lord Soth|25c|x}").

#### Lord Soth of Sithicus

#### Villain of Two Worlds

Lord Soth features in adventures in both the Dragonlance and Ravenloft campaign settings. If you want to give Soth a unique relationship to the Domains of Dread, consider these possibilities:

- The nature of Soth's curse creates aspects of the death knight in both Krynn and Sithicus. When Soth broods in his fortress in Krynn, he remains active in Sithicus. When the evil god, Takhisis, calls for his service on Krynn, Soth enters a magical torpor in his fortress in Sithicus.

- Soth traveled from Krynn to Sithicus and is destined to return. If he leaves Sithicus, the domain might be destroyed, or a villain like Inza Kulchevich might take over.

- Soth was trapped in Sithicus, escaped, and was trapped again, perpetuating a cycle of despair.

Soth's story and where characters encounter him are for you to detail in your adventures.

#### Lord Soth's Tale

Lord Soth was given a quest to save the people of his world, Krynn, from the wrath of the gods. Possessed by baseless paranoia and possessiveness of his elf wife, Soth forsook that quest, and the world was devastated in a great Cataclysm. In punishment, Soth was resurrected as a death knight and, for centuries, brooded on his disgrace in the charred ruins of his fortress, Dargaard Keep.

When the evil god Takhisis whispered to Soth, promising to restore his glory and avenge him against the heroic knights who banished him long ago, Soth answered and served Takhisis. During this time, he called on his undead servants, including the ghost of the knight Caradoc. Seeking to forge a new knighthood of loyal dead, Soth became obsessed with Dragon Highlord Kitiara Uth Matar and charged Caradoc with claiming her soul. When Kitiara died in battle, Caradoc refused to capture her spirit unless Soth bargained with Takhisis to restore Caradoc to life. Enraged, Soth tried to destroy Caradoc as the Mists rose around them.

When the Mists faded, Soth found himself in the domain of {@book Barovia|RHW|2}. For a time, he found an ally in {@creature Strahd von Zarovich|RHW|Count Strahd von Zarovich}, but the two villains couldn't abide one another for long. Soth eventually pursued rumors of his treacherous servant, Caradoc, into the Mists. When the Mists parted again, the domain of Sithicus spread before him.

All is well in Tepest. Fields shimmer with bountiful harvests, and horned village children recite sing-song rhymes. The people of Viktal do what they must to survive, using tradition to cloak their fear and complicity in a cycle of murder. #### Mother's Sight

A mile outside Viktal, in a field apart from the surrounding farms, stands Mother's Sight: an array of rune-etched standing stones. These stones rise as tall as thirty feet, and several have pieces that defy gravity. Within the central and largest stone floats a disk reminiscent of a great eye.

### Mother Lorinda

{@i Darklord Who Demands Adoration}

What precocious moppets you are! Little ones who show Mother love get rewarded, but bad children get punished. Which sorts of tots are you?

Mother Lorinda desires a family over all else, not realizing she's destroyed every family she's ever had. A creator of monstrosities, this ancient green hag obsesses over unnatural maternalistic pursuits.

Over generations, Tepest's villagers have come to view Lorinda not as a powerful witch, but as a god of the land and fertility called Mother. Lorinda doesn't dissuade her fragile mortal followers from their baseless cult practices. The villagers provide willing sacrifices for the hag's flawed magical attempts to create life, and their adoration gradually soothes the violent, magical outbursts that follow her failures.

Lorinda's walking tower of bones, the Gurgyl, ranges across Tepest. Those who seek her here find a distracted but deceptively cunning hag clinging to grotesque dolls of her family. She laments the loss of her sisters, Laveeda and Leticia, never acknowledging that they remain alive as her prisoners. She often speaks of her daughter, Laoirse (pronounced "LUR-shuh")—the name she uses over and over again for each of her monstrous creations.

When Lorinda visits Viktal, she appears in her illusory guise as Mother, an ancient, powerful woman wearing a mothlike cloak and bearing a branch with burning limbs. She calls on the people to prove their love for her through sacrifice, whether of food and crafts, their left eyes, or their lives. Sometimes her arrival is sudden and terrifying, such as when she magically appears to crawl from one of her followers' empty eye sockets.

Lorinda seeks new ways to create a permanent family. This might involve great sacrifices, stolen fey magic, strange adoptions, or all manner of supernatural bargains.

#### Mother Lorinda

#### Mother Lorinda's Tale

The sisters Lorinda, Laveeda, and Leticia Mindefisk lived desperate lives at the wooded edge of a terrible land. Their brutish family claimed the girls were unnatural fey in disguise and forced them to toil constantly. After years of harsh treatment from their cruel father and brothers, the hopeless sisters snapped and killed an abusive traveler during a chance encounter. They butchered the body and served the remains to their unwitting family. Thus began a cycle of murder and cannibalism that persisted for years.

The Mindefisk sisters' crimes were finally revealed when they all fell in love with a charming stranger. They squabbled, and unwilling to relent or share, they murdered the stranger in full view of their horrified family. With nothing left to lose, the sisters killed the rest of their family as the Mists rose around them.

The horror of the Mindefisk sisters only grew in the Domains of Dread. Finding themselves in Tepest, the sisters discovered their new nature as hags. Over generations, mortals and fey alike came to know them as fickle miracle workers. Despairing families came to them often, willing to trade much for {@race hexblood|RHW} children born from the hags' magic.

Eventually, Lorinda grew fascinated by others' pursuit of family. She entreated her sisters to help her create a child, but Laveeda and Leticia refused, loathing the idea of any one of them having greater happiness than the others.

Defying her sisters, Lorinda assembled a monstrosity of animal parts and foul magic in secret. With her new daughter's help, the hag trapped her sisters inside their magic cauldron and stole their powers. Yet Lorinda's joy with her creation was short—the monster's magic quickly waned, and it rotted away. Ever since, Lorinda has sought to create a family of her own, producing countless monstrosities in experiments and approaching the villagers of Viktal in her guise as Mother. Over generations, this has given rise to the cult of the Mother in Viktal, but it has brought Lorinda no closer to attaining the lasting family connection she covets.

#### Oselo

### Chakuna

{@i Darklord Who Hunts to Survive}

You seem capable, but do you have the heart of a hunter?

Chakuna is Valachan's deadliest hunter, a youthful werepanther with the intensity and calm of a veteran executioner. Few can elude her cold cunning, deadly traps, and packs of feline allies.

Chakuna is no mere murderer. She hunts because she must to protect the people of Oselo from a more terrible fate. To that end, she prefers to target strangers in her land, forcing them to participate in the Trial of Hearts. She relishes the chance to hunt the proud and disrespectful, as well as the skilled and unpredictable. Chakuna knows no mercy, though, and even those she comes to respect eventually face her blade. She spares the people of Oselo alone, even as her peoples' fear of her grows.

#### Chakuna

#### Chakuna's Tale

For untold generations, Darklord Urik von Kharkov ruled Valachan from Castle Pantara and hunted the people of Valachan for sport. Yearly, the Darklord's hunts grew in carnage, with his favorite prey being the shape-shifters of Oselo. Von Kharkov viewed himself as the domain's greatest hunter, a title he confirmed every time he slaughtered one of Oselo's supernatural warriors.

Chakuna was a werepanther warrior of Oselo and, like most of her people, was a skillful hunter. Seeing that von Kharkov eventually would slay her entire village, she swore to save Oselo. Realizing it would take a monster to slay a monster, she left her people and sought the deepest secrets of her deadly homeland. When she returned, she volunteered to let von Kharkov hunt her. During the murderous game that followed, Chakuna burned Castle Pantara and took the former Darklord's head.

Valachan is no mundane land, though. A Domain of Dread demands a Darklord, and Valachan will be ruled only by its greatest hunter. Refusing to let von Kharkov return, Chakuna made a pact with the domain itself. Within the depths of the mazelike Yaguara Mountains, Chakuna ripped her own heart from her chest and consumed the heart of Urik von Kharkov. Doing so, she stole von Kharkov's powers. Yet to retain them, she must feed the land constantly with the blood of the hunted. If she doesn't, the plants and animals of Valachan grow murderous and howl for von Kharkov's return. So Chakuna continues a cycle of bloodshed, embracing the violence of her peoples' onetime oppressor to ensure her bitter domination of her vicious realm.

##### Trial of Hearts Rules

Visitors to Valachan likely are forced to participate in the Trial of Hearts, a dangerous game where the domain's Darklord hunts them. On the night of the full moon, {@creature Chakuna|RHW} selects those she considers worthy to participate. At the Pantara Lodge, she shows her guests every courtesy, provides them with a map of the domain, and sets out these rules for the trial:

- Participants can divide into small groups or go it alone.

- Once groups are established at the trial's start, alliances between contenders aren't permitted.

- Participants must reach one of two shrines: either on Kiru Island or between the lakes called the Scars atop Anquara Plateau.

- Participants can leave the Pantara Lodge at dawn. The following dusk, Chakuna pursues the contenders with hunting partners of her choice, attempting to slay any participants she encounters outside a shrine.

- Only one person or group can occupy a shrine.

- Participants can kill one another for any reason—particularly to take a shrine.

- The trial doesn't stop for any reason until Chakuna arrives at both shrines and acknowledges any winners there.

- The Darklord's servants escort winners to the village of Shuaran, and from there, out of Valachan.

- Losers rot where they fall.

- Rule breakers are killed.

Chakuna wants the trial to be challenging. If she catches a competitor too soon, she might release them if they sacrifice an ally, a limb, or a valuable weapon. Anyone who dies in the trial feeds the domain's bloodlust—even those who fall to a threat other than the Darklord herself.

##### Valachan Exploration

Valachan is a dense and deadly wilderness. When creating adventures here, refer to {@book chapter 2|XDMG|1|Running Exploration} of the {@book Dungeon Master's Guide|XDMG} for details on travel and journeys with stages.

Characters use the rules in the "{@book Travel Pace|XPHB|1|Travel Pace}" section of the {@book Player's Handbook|XPHB}. Valachan's forests prevent a party from traveling at faster than a Normal pace, and their density makes them {@variantrule Difficult Terrain|XPHB}, reducing travel pace by half. This means most characters can move through the domain at a Normal pace of 1½ miles per hour and 12 miles per day.

###### Flying

Difficult Terrain slows flying creatures in Valachan, due to the strong winds above the forest canopy. Flying creatures are also easy to spot, attracting the attention of {@creature Chimera|XMM|Chimeras}, {@creature Pteranodon|XMM|Pteranodons}, and condor-like {@creature Roc|XMM|Rocs}. Chakuna might stipulate that flight is off-limits during the Trial of Hearts. If she does, the Mists aid in enforcing this rule.

###### Navigation

It's easy to get lost in Valachan's jungles. Unless they use gorges and waterways for guidance, characters must make {@dc 15} Wisdom ({@skill Survival|XPHB}) checks to avoid getting lost. Those who fail spend {@dice 1d4} hours wandering and risk encountering deadly creatures (see "{@area Trial of Hearts|295|x}" for suggestions). Characters should make this check once during each stage of a journey, or after dramatic or disorienting encounters.

###### Trial Route: Kiru Island

An approximately 20-mile route from the Pantara Lodge to Kiru Island runs along the gorge south of the lodge to the Eirubamba River, then along the coast to Kiru Island. At a Normal pace, this route takes a day and a half (13 hours) to complete. Roughly 10 miles of this route follows the river. Characters who use a raft can move at Fast pace along the river. Rapids make the river {@variantrule Difficult Terrain|XPHB}, but even so, moving at this pace reduces the journey to just over a day (11 hours).

###### Trial Route: The Scars

Participants in the Trial of Hearts who travel from the Pantara Lodge to the Scars on Anquara Plateau have a route of approximately 20 miles if they follow the Shadowtaker Lakes east to the river called Killer's Road, then ascend Anquara Plateau. Characters can follow most of this route at a Normal pace, but 2 miles involve climbing into the mountains—a Slow pace through Difficult Terrain, reducing movement to 1 mile per hour. This route takes over a day and a half (14 hours) to complete.

###### Rival

If the characters reached the shrine in less than 15 hours, they are the first ones there. After {@dice 2d4} hours, the pirate group arrives at the Kiru Island shrine, and the lizardfolk group arrives at the Scars shrine. Both groups are missing any members who died in previous encounters, but they're {@variantrule Hostile [Attitude]|XPHB|Hostile} and fight the party. If the characters took longer than 15 hours, this rival group arrives first.

###### Conclusion

An hour after the group contends with its rivals, Chakuna arrives. If the characters followed the rules of the Trial of Hearts, she pronounces them victors and upholds her end of the bargain—helping them leave Valachan. If they broke the trial's rules in even the slightest way, Chakuna knows it and fights them. In either case, each character earns a bonus 2,100 XP.

## Other Domains of Dread

None can say how many domains drift through the Mists. This section hints at the multitudes of additional Domains of Dread. Detail and explore them in your adventures, or use them as examples when creating your own domains using {@book chapter 4|RHW|4}.

### The Agency

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Everything inside the office of the Agency is a monotone gray. Anyone who passes the frosted glass door into this single-room domain is expected by Flimira "Flintlock" Vhage, the detective agency's owner. From here, Flimira collects cryptic correspondence relating to mysteries across the Domains of Dread. She enlists agents to investigate these cases, then report back to her. However, she never reveals her own past as a detective-turned-criminal, her involvement in every case her agency investigates, or that the Agency exists within her mind.

### Bluetspur

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The entity that would become the God-Brain of Bluetspur was an illithid elder brain whose existence-violating experiments revealed a malignant truth of the multiverse. This secret manifested in the elder brain's mind as an alien disease. Terrified of learning this pernicious, devouring knowledge themselves, the other illithid elder brains banished their peer from existence. In actuality, the Dark Powers claimed the afflicted elder brain and granted it an alien domain. Now called the God-Brain by the mind flayers and stranger entities that serve it, this elder brain pursues radical methods of saving itself from the thoughts destroying it. Its servants claim trespassers and beings from other domains, violating their minds with their bizarre experiments.

### Carnival

- Darklords:

- Hallmarks:

The Carnival is a wandering domain that appears within other Domains of Dread. Before the Carnival appears, mysterious fliers blow into communities near where it's about to arrive. When it arrives, the Carnival sets up a surreal wonderland of entertainment and oddities staffed by performers, outcasts, and cursed individuals from countless lands. Attractions include grand shows at the big top, sideshow performances, and the Hall of Horrors. The Carnival's mysterious leader is Isolde, a good but wrathful warrior of the {@book Feywild|XDMG|5|Feywild} who's cursed to lead the Carnival even as she hunts an elusive foe across the Domains of Dread. She carries the bladelike {@i Nepenthe}, a weapon that hungers for blood. Although driven by vengeance and painful memories, Isolde protects the Carnival at all costs.

### Cyre 1313, the Mourning Rail

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The disaster known as the Mourning numbers among the greatest tragedies to befall the world of Eberron—a mysterious calamity that killed nearly everyone in the land of Cyre. In the nation's capital of Metrol, some citizens foresaw the coming devastation and sought to escape on lightning rails: elemental-powered engines capable of pulling trains of passenger carts. As scared innocents packed Metrol's last lightning rail, known as Cyre 1313, the evacuation was delayed at the demand of a late-arriving VIP. Hundreds were forced from passenger carts to admit and maintain the secrecy of this last passenger and their retinue. When the lightning rail finally departed, it was too late—the Mourning overtook the train and its escapees. But even as it did so, the Mists claimed Cyre 1313 and all aboard it. Now, the last lightning rail from Metrol hurtles through the Mists as a traveling domain.

Those on board fear the disaster pursuing them, the mysterious passenger seated in the train's foremost cart, and the necrotic energy now infusing the engine's elemental spirit. Yet none of the passengers realizes that their endless quest for escape is pointless, as Cyre 1313 carries only the dead.

### Forlorn

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Tristen ApBlanc was born amid tragedy, the son of a vampire father and a human noble. Fearful villagers murdered his parents, leaving the boy to be adopted by druids. But during his teenage years, his {@race dhampir|RHW} nature revealed itself. When the druids discovered the youth's hunger for blood, they cast him out. Tristen grew enraged and slaughtered them, draining them all. The sacred waters of the druids' rituals had infused their blood, though, making it poison to the dhampir. Tristen died with his adopted family, but as the Mists closed in around their sacred stone circle, he rose as a ghost.

Unexpectedly, with the dawn, the Darklord's dhampir body was restored. Now, he lives by day, a perpetually young, charming, invention-obsessed dhampir dwelling in Castle Tristenoira, the smoking fortress his goblin servants built over the druid circle of his onetime family. At night, though, the dhampir dies a painful death, then rises in his spectral form to scour all that is green and vibrant from his land.

### Ghastria

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

A notorious hedonist, Marquis Stezen d'Polarno enjoyed popularity among his noble peers but craved immortality. A mysterious artist offered him eternity by painting Stezen's portrait on a magical canvas. But the artist didn't mention that the painting would strip Stezen of his love of life and natural charm. Despite his dulled existence, the marquis enjoys one reprieve: Once every season, when he shows his portrait to an audience, it consumes their souls and refreshes his thrill for life. Stezen's artistic predations captured the attention of the Dark Powers, which drew his lands into the Mists. Now, Ghastria is a fertile island that, like its lord, lacks an essential vim—except for once per season, when vigor fleetingly returns.

### G'henna

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Born of a Barovian family, Yagno Petrovna went missing on the slopes of Mount Ghakis as a youth. As a violent storm rose, he took shelter in a mysterious ruin and was found weeks later wandering the hills, babbling about an amber idol and the god he'd discovered: Zhakata the Provider and the Devourer. His family sought to help him, but when they learned Yagno had been secretly sacrificing people to his fictitious god, they chased him into the Mists. When he emerged, the domain of G'henna sprawled before him.

Life is hard in G'henna, a rocky land home to fierce, ravenous animals. The domain's starving people worship the bestial god Zhakata and regularly travel to the cathedral-city of Zhukar. There, they offer their crops in sacrifice and hear Zhakata's will through the revered prophet Yagno Petrovna.

### I'Cath

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

In her youth, Tsien Chiang accidentally slew her gold dragon mentor to create a magic, wish-granting bell capable of driving invaders from her homeland. Her scheme worked, and the people made Chiang their queen. Over time, though, the people bristled under Chiang's rule and rose up in revolt. Slaughter and loss on both sides led Chiang to ring her magic bell again, but this time the bell cracked, and the Mists spilled forth. When they faded, Tsien Chiang was the ruler of I'Cath, a labyrinthine, nightmare city where the people sleep endlessly. In their dreams, they toil forever in a fantasy version of the city, working to maintain the perfectionism of their harsh queen.

### Invidia

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Gabrielle Aderre is convinced that her son, Malocchio, is destined for greatness. From her estate outside the village of Karina, she employs an endless string of servants to provide Malocchio with the best possible upbringing. Inevitably, though, every servant flees or vanishes, either as a result of Gabrielle's unreasonable expectations or the deadly tricks and accidents that frequently occur around the child. Gabrielle isn't content to leave her son's grand destiny to chance, though. Using her precious bone spirit board, she calls on supernatural forces to guide and protect her son. Spirits, angels, fiends, and worse answer her summons—but as long as they chart Malocchio's path to glory, the Darklord eagerly accepts their gifts.

### Kalidnay

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The city of Kalidnay was once the seat of power of Kalid-Ma, one of the monstrous sorcerer-kings of Athas. Thakok-An, the greatest of Kalid-Ma's enforcers, desired the sorcerer-king so greatly that when she learned of a plot against her master, she didn't reveal it. Instead, she performed a magical rite to grant Kalid-Ma even greater power, sacrificing her own family to do so. Her ritual went wrong, though, and life-draining magic washed over Kalidnay, destroying the city and draining life from nearly all its residents. Awaking amid the ruins, Thakok-An found Kalidnay surrounded by an endless sea of silt and mist. Worse, rather than being empowered, Kalid-Ma languished in a cursed torpor, the sorcerer-king's vast magic bleeding away.

Now Thakok-An obsesses over her sleeping king. From Kalidnay's survivors, she's created a new cult of servants. Following Thakok-An's wishes, these templars condemn trespassers in Kalidnay to ritual combat in the arena, where the lives of the slain sustain the secretly withering sorcerer-king.

### Keening

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The forbidding land of Keening is dominated by Mount Lament, at whose base lies the village of Anwrtyn. The people of Anwrtyn deafen themselves so they won't hear the shrieking of the banshee Tristessa, a mournful spirit who roams Mount Lament and whose wail carries through the night. In life, Tristessa dwelled under Mount Arak in Tepest (detailed earlier in {@book this book|RHW|2}), but she was exiled for crimes against her people. In death, she endlessly seeks to reunite with the family slain by her misdeeds.

### Klorr

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Klorr is the end of worlds. Here, shattered islands drift through a misty netherworld, caught in a swirling, clockwork spiral that ends at the unignorable, burning eye called Klorr. Thirteen stars orbit this sunlike sphere, one winking out every hour. Each time one of the stars dies, one of the domain's ruined islands is drawn into Klorr and consumed by flames. Afterward, each other island wrenches ahead, then halts, one hour closer to the same doom. When all thirteen stars have winked out, they return hourly to the sky.

Those who dwell on the crumbling islands trapped in the domain count the hours until their end. Few know how they came to Klorr or when new islands get added to the cycle, only that the Mists closed in and doomed them. Amid the realm's surreal skies float the ruins of lost domains—among them, a city of skulls, a realm overrun by dogs, and an array of bloody, iron arenas. Echoes of domains that exist elsewhere in the Domains of Dread also drift here, including at least one duplicate of Castle Ravenloft. Those cast away amid this orderly apocalypse grow ever more desperate to defy the doomsday clock and the will of a hidden Darklord, the obsessed clockmaker named Klorr.

### Markovia

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Dr. Frantisek Markov is a genius—but less so than yesterday. His Markov Formula grants him unparalleled intellect, but it insidiously steals more than it gives, making him increasingly dull-witted and bestial in form. Despairing, the doctor fled to a tropical island he dubbed Markovia, where he tests new versions of his formula on the local fauna in hopes of recovering his waning genius. As a result of these tests, animals across Markovia now possess sapience and have been deluded into worshipping Markov as their god.

### Nightmare Lands

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Any who sleep might become forever trapped in the Nightmare Lands, a phantasmagoric realm whose features shift endlessly. Those who visit and escape speak of malicious wildernesses; the empty city called Nod; and uncountable drifting spheres, each containing a stranger's unending nightmare. Insidious entities called the Nightmare Court rule the domain. No one knows how many members make up the court, but they include the tragically graceful Ghost Dancer, the tomb-bound Hypnos, the witch Mullonga, the trickster Morpheus, and the embodiment of terror known as the Nightmare Man. These beings, artisans of nightmares, visit terrors on any whose sleeping minds brush against the domain.

The Nightmare Court's members share one commonality: All are the living nightmares of Caroline Dinwiddy, a potent psychic who has repressed memories of her own heartless deeds. These memories torment her sleeping mind, creating the Nightmare Court. Deep within the City of Nod, inside a warped reimagining of the clinic where she once worked, Caroline sleeps without waking, refusing to face the terrors her dreams unleash on innocents across the Domains of Dread and beyond.

### Niranjan

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The Ashram of Niranjan is a vibrant vihara, or monastery, on a misty chain of islands. Here the elderly scholar Niranjan leads ascetics in practicing Ramsana, a way of life prioritizing nonattachment to the material world. In truth, Niranjan is Sarthak, a wicked bronze dragon who sends agents into the Mists bearing his philosophical writings. These works promise escape and peace to any who adopt their teachings and search the Mists for their source. Those who come to the ashram must divest themselves of worldly goods, which become part of Sarthak's hidden hoard. Sarthak then helps his victims enter a trance that causes their souls to slip away from their bodies. Sarthak consumes these souls and replaces them with shadows, leaving the victims' bodies under his control.

### Nova Vaasa

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

An unparalleled warrior, Myar Hiregaard united the nomadic tribes of the vast plains of Nova Vaasa. But, while respected as a soldier, Hiregaard made a poor peacetime leader. When brutal games could no longer keep her interest, she incited hostilities between two of her vassal tribes, then led her own forces to crush them. Subtly, she did this again and again.

After Hiregaard's greatest massacre, the Mists enfolded all of Nova Vaasa, splitting the Darklord's personality in two when they did. Now she rules her people with strict fairness—but when her bloodlust is piqued, she transforms into the raging knight called Malkan and sows discord across the plains.

### Odaire

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The toymaker Giuseppe had his wish for a family granted when his creation, the marionette Figlio, came to life. A proud father, the toymaker presented his son to all the people of his village, Odaire. The local children loved Figlio, but their skeptical parents said the marionette was nothing but a toy. Over time, this doubt enraged Figlio, and the marionette convinced Giuseppe to craft siblings for him. Then, when the time was right, Giuseppe's creations did away with all the adults in Odaire.

Claimed by the Mists, Odaire is a village populated only by children and ruled by Figlio the {@creature Carrionette|RHW} (see {@area chapter 5|39b|x}), who now calls himself Maligno.

### Richemulot

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The once-beautiful cities of Richemulot crumble as season after season of the Gnawing Plague afflicts the domain. Countless commoners and aristocrats have died, causing rule to settle with the vain noble Jacqueline Renier. Unbeknownst to the people, Jacqueline is a wererat whose servants create and spread the plague. She loathes what her domain has become but refuses to cede power back to the people by ending the ongoing disaster. Jacqueline's obedient state police, the Casques Silencieux, enforce martial law and quarantines across the domain.

### Rider's Bridge

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Nearly every domain knows some version of the apparition called the Headless Rider. It appears as a mercenary in dark armor in Mordent, a ghostly cataphract in Har'Akir, and a mutated centaur in Lamordia. But in each incarnation, certain details remain true: the rider lacks its head, it appears on a prominent bridge, and it decapitates victims as it endlessly searches for its own head. Anyone who escapes the Headless Rider might find a different domain on the opposite side of the spirit's bridge. (See "{@area Dullahan|3a1|x}" in {@book chapter 5|RHW|5} for more details.)

### Risibilos

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The Risibilos is a small music hall, similar to those found in any decent-sized city. Its lord, Doerdon, was once a king—one so thoroughly humorless that he forbade his subjects the privilege of laughter, on pain of death. He now finds himself cursed to entertain others—a task he is utterly unqualified for. Fortunately, the Mists delivered him a partner, a ventriloquist's dummy carved in the likeness of {@creature Strahd von Zarovich|RHW}. The eerie dummy has a mind of its own, insisting it is the real Strahd and that the creature currently sitting on the throne of Castle Ravenloft is a mere impostor. It rages at the audience and makes audacious promises to any who will help it regain its station. Anyone with the slightest inkling of who Strahd is finds this hilarious. Their peals of laughter are agony to Doerdon's ears.

### Scaena

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Lemont Sediam Juste fancied himself a serious playwright, and he achieved popular, if not critical, acclaim in {@book Dementlieu|RHW|2} for his works of grisly horror. But he craved respectability, and with his new play {@i Apparitions}, Lemont believed he would find it. The night of the premiere, when the audience signaled their boredom, the playwright was crestfallen. His supporters wanted blood, so he gave them what they craved.

By the play's end, Lemont had joined the drama and brutally murdered every member of the cast while the crowd roared their approval. As the show ended, the playhouse broke from Dementlieu, and Scaena was formed. Comprising a single playhouse, the domain can create any reality Lemont desires on its stage. The Darklord's immersive performances become somewhat predictable, though, as they always end in slaughter.

### Sea of Sorrows

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

The murderous pirate Pietra van Riese, captain of the {@i Relentless}, had an unsavory reputation for attaching her captives to ropes and dragging them through the water until they drowned. She never removed the detritus of her victims, even though some returned to life as zombies. Rival captains ultimately sank the {@i Relentless}, but death couldn't keep Pietra. She awoke in the Sea of Sorrows, water in her lungs and sea creatures making their homes in her flesh. Her crew stirred with her, now fish-eaten corpses. When the captain sought to speak with them, her voice emerged from their mouths.

The {@i Relentless} sails a domain that can overlap any body of water in any other domain. Some domains border the Sea of Sorrows, while others call these mysterious waters by other names. Those who venture into the Mists by ship might enter the sea's endless debris-choked expanse of eerie beasts and shifting islands, including the following:

- Blaustein

- Dominia

- Isle of the Ravens

- The Lighthouse

- Vigilant's Bluff

### Souragne

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

In society, Anton Misroi presented himself as an upstanding gentleman who served as a prison warden. But within the walls of the prison, he became a sadist who believed righteousness was on his side. When his torturous punishments finally drove the inmates to rise up, the bloody riot that ensued drew the attentions of the Dark Powers. During the uprising, Anton drowned in the swamps surrounding the prison. But he rose again soon after, an undead warden in search of inmates.

Beyond Anton's prison, alligator-filled swamps cover the domain of Souragne right up to the sinking settlement of Port d'Elhour and Marais d'Tarascon, a village where above-ground crypts outnumber the residences of the living.

### Staunton Bluffs

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Eerily faceless mercenary regiments sweep the countryside of Staunton Bluffs. Burning villages and killing helpless residents, they push ever eastward toward Castle Stonecrest, hereditary home of the Bleysmith family. Teresa Bleysmith, spurred by jealousy of her brother, Torrence, gave her family's foes the intelligence they needed to raid Staunton Bluffs. The attack was never supposed to go so far, but the duplicitous mercenaries stormed through the countryside to take Castle Stonecrest within a day. Teresa survived the attack—but when she surveyed the damage done and found her family dead by her own designs, she threw herself from the bluffs. Now she haunts her own domain, where she repeatedly relives the day of her betrayal.

### Tovag

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Notorious across the planes, the vampire Kas was once the champion of the lich {@book Vecna|XDMG|8|Vecna}. Wielding the mighty sword that bears his name, he betrayed his master, and the resulting battle supposedly destroyed them both. In truth, Vecna escaped and grew in power over ages and across worlds. Kas, though, was claimed by the Mists, and in his wasteland domain of Tovag, he believes his war with Vecna rages on. Patrols of prisoner-soldiers under undead commanders scour the land, dragooning strangers to serve in Kas's armies and to manufacture bizarre war machines. When Kas deems the time right, he sends his forces into the Mists, believing that Vecna's realm lies just beyond. Invariably, those troops never return, leaving the vampire to rage, rebuild his forces, and continue his search for the {@item Sword of Kas|XDMG} ({@book detailed|XDMG|6|Magic Items A-Z} in the {@book Dungeon Master's Guide|XDMG}), which he considers his key to victory.

At least once, the Dark Powers have released Kas from his domain to torment their onetime prisoner Vecna. Inevitably, when Kas fails to defeat his ancient rival, the Mists return him to his domain to rage for another era.

### Zherisia

- Darklord:

- Hallmarks:

Each day, the city of Paridon in Zherisia erupts in riots over food scarcity, taxation, and the disappearance of dozens of citizens every night. At least one murderer stalks Paridon: the ancient doppelganger Sodo, which has impersonated so many people that it now finds it impossible to hold a form for more than a few days at a time. As Sodo's flesh runs like hot wax, it staves off dissolution by consuming the organs of humanoids. Paridon's streets serve as the Darklord's hunting ground, and those who enter the sewers risk attracting the notice of countless insectile horrors, including {@creature Carrion Stalker|RHW|carrion stalkers} (see {@area chapter 5|39a|x}) and their monstrous Hive Queen.

### Random Domains

Characters might find themselves in an unexpected domain when the Mists initially carry them to the Domains of Dread or if they aimlessly enter the Mists. When this occurs, roll on or choose a result from the Random Domains of Dread table to determine where the characters emerge. Domains marked with an asterisk (*) are featured domains detailed earlier in this book.

The Random Domains of Dread table also lists what tarokka cards are aligned with and opposed to each domain. Cards marked with a dagger (†) are also aligned with another Domain of Dread. Randomly determine which domain the Mists transport characters to when this card is drawn.
