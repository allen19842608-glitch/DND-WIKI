## Key Plot Points

The following information is key to this adventure's story:

- #### Venger's Schemes
  
  The characters receive a mysterious missive from Venger (see x), a fiendish mage who invites them to plumb the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents in search of treasure. In reality, Venger wishes to test whether the characters would be useful tools for his schemes.
- #### Escape the Tomb
  
  The characters venture into the tomb and discover many fallen heroes who previously tried to claim Venger's treasure. If the characters survive, they can keep the magic items they find, and they pique Venger's interest. If they die, their bones will lie in the tomb forever.

## Adventure Background

The fiendish, immortal mage Venger weaves interlocking schemes across the D&D multiverse to amass power and thwart his enemies. Some of Venger's schemes involve allying with—or opposing—heroes he manipulates to his own ends.

To identify heroes who might prove useful in future plots, Venger has built tombs across the multiverse as testing grounds. When heroes' deeds catch his attention, he invites those heroes to claim the treasure inside the tombs. Venger often challenges heroes to explore numerous tombs before he's convinced of their usefulness.

The offer of treasure is real—though Venger considers the treasures in his tombs inconsequential, adventurers might find them worth the risk. But the tombs are deadly, and they've become the final resting place for many adventurers who've failed Venger's challenges.

Among the earliest tombs Venger built is the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents. It's situated within a cave on the Material Plane near an intersection with the Shadowfell, so strange and gloomy environs envelop the tomb. The tomb holds deadly monsters and traps. Surviving the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents is the first step toward earning Venger's respect.

### Adventure Hooks

For this adventure, your characters need to know only that a powerful individual has challenged them to explore the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents in search of treasure. You might make it clear that Venger has invited them, or you could obscure his identity, allowing the characters to deduce the tomb's creator while they explore or even once they've completed the adventure.

If you want to further entice the characters, use one of the following adventure hooks to draw them into the story:

- ##### A Deadly Rivalry
  
  The characters are embroiled in competition with another group and wish to prove their superior skills. Perhaps the characters are working for one of the magical factions described in x and wish to claim the tomb's treasure before another faction does. Or perhaps the characters have caught wind that rivals who compete with them for quests are also headed toward the tomb, and the characters hope to claim its treasure first. Regardless of the details, the characters can claim treasure—and bragging rights—if they survive the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents.
- ##### Impress a Patron
  
  The characters are cultivating a relationship with a powerful patron who might fund their continued adventures. Perhaps the characters want to impress a potential patron—such as one of the group patrons described in x—or they're trying to cement a fledgling relationship with a patron. Either way, the patron loves magical lore and tales of adventure. Emerging victorious from the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents is sure to get the patron's attention and prove the characters' capabilities.

## Beginning the Adventure

The characters receive a message from Venger. This might reach the characters in a nonmagical way, such as a letter delivered by a messenger, or it might reach them via magic such as a Magic Mouth or Programmed Illusion spell.

For players excited to play an adventure involving Venger, consider beginning with a *Programmed Illusion* spell (spell save DC 19) that activates when the characters reach the forest in which the tomb is located. When the illusion activates, the characters see a winged, one-horned fiend astride a flying black horse. The fiend swoops down from above and delivers the message in a deep, booming voice before flying away again.

If the journey to the tomb is more than a short distance away, include directions in this message as necessary:

"Would-be heroes! Should you desire fame and treasure, heed this challenge. My Tomb of the Shadow Serpents lies nearby, built within a cave of unrelieved gloom. Seek out the stone doors that bear a serpent; within lies what you seek."

"I eagerly await heroes worthy of claiming the tomb's riches. Many die terrible deaths within, but perhaps you shall be different. Test your mettle in my tomb, and perhaps one day we shall meet again to our mutual benefit."

## Tomb of the Shadow Serpents

The Tomb of the Shadow Serpents has served Venger for generations as a testing ground for adventurers. Before inviting adventurers to brave it, Venger resets or repairs the tomb's features and replenishes its treasure to ensure there are always challenges and rewards for heroes who survive it. He never gives another thought to those who die here, leaving their bodies to litter the tomb.

Three Shadows lurk in this room, each hidden in one of the sarcophagi. As soon as a character ventures west of the serpent statue, the shadows emerge and begin combat.

The shadows arose from the bodies of adventurers Venger killed for betraying him. The mage then bound the shadows to this room. In a cruel act of irony, Venger built elaborate sarcophagi for the adventurers' bodies and placed them here, taunting the shadows with the prospect of eternal rest.

##### Serpent Statue

Venger built the serpent statue and imbued it with magic he drew from the Shadowfell. As a result, the statue causes this room to behave as if the room were on that plane. It also causes the shadows to take on a serpentine appearance.

A character who enters this room must succeed on a DC 10 Wisdom saving throw or be affected by despair. Roll on the Shadowfell Despair table in the Dungeon Master's Guide to determine the form the despair takes.

The statue has AC 15, HP 18, and Vulnerability to Radiant damage. If the statue is reduced to 0 HP, the Shadowfell stops affecting this room, and any despair effects on the characters end immediately.

A character who takes the Study action to examine the statue and succeeds on a DC 15 Intelligence (Arcana) check understands how the statue is affecting the room and surmises that its connection to the Shadowfell gives it Vulnerability to Radiant damage.

## Conclusion

Once the characters are finished exploring the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents, award each character 100 XP for completing the adventure. The characters should have enough XP to advance to level 2 by the end of the adventure. If you're using a method of level advancement that doesn't rely on XP, the characters gain a level after defeating the shadows in the sepulcher.

If the characters survive the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents, Venger notices their prowess. He tracks the characters throughout their adventuring careers and might challenge them to explore other tombs he's created throughout the multiverse.

Dungeons in several published D&D adventures can be easily repurposed as tombs that Venger challenges the characters to explore. The temple in the level 4 adventure "The Will of Orcus" in 1, for example, could be reimagined as such a tomb, with the cultists of the demon lord Orcus instead becoming sycophantic followers of Venger. The Tomb of the Nine Gods in 7, meant for level 9 adventurers, could similarly be reimagined as a complex created by Venger instead of the lich Acererak. The Tomb of Wayward Souls in 7, another dungeon created by Acererak, is appropriate for level 16 characters. You can also create new tombs that Venger challenges the characters to explore.

At your discretion, after the characters have survived the dangers of numerous tombs, Venger might offer the characters mysterious jobs such as finding an item, stopping or delivering a shipment of goods, or protecting a public figure. These schemes should feed into Venger's attempts to thwart the League of Malevolence, an odious assemblage of power-hungry villains led by Venger's former lackey, the human mage Kelek. The characters shouldn't trust Venger, but they might accept his jobs (which always pay well) out of curiosity or as part of a plan to double-cross him.
